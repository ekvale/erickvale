About

I'm a data scientist and full-stack developer who builds systems for a living and side projects for fun. Honestly the line between the two is thinner than it should be. Most of what I make ends up on this site: games, tools, archives, little simulations. I'd rather ship something than let it sit in a folder on my laptop.

By trade, I work at the intersection of clinical informatics, health data infrastructure, and public health surveillance. That means building the technical systems that let public health agencies, health system research consortia, and community organizations get timely, accurate health data on populations that existing surveillance tends to miss, including people experiencing homelessness, incarceration, and housing instability.

Stratum Health is where that work lives full time: a federated population health data pipeline built on the OMOP CDM, OHDSI tooling, and a Django data product layer, in the same architecture used by the Minnesota Electronic Health Record Consortium and similar statewide collaboratives.

Before this, I worked across nonprofit, public sector, and collaborative data settings, with the same focus the whole time: making health data infrastructure actually work for the communities most affected by health disparities and least represented in the systems built to measure them.

What the pipeline demo actually shows

This site still hosts the technical walkthrough behind Stratum Health, if you want to see how it actually works.

A working OMOP CDM implementation

The pipeline walkthrough steps through a live federated data environment: OMOP domain tables and concept mapping, PPRL tokenization, deduplication, administrative data enrichment, cohort queries, and small-cell suppression, all the way to stratified prevalence estimates.

Privacy-preserving record linkage

The site implements HMAC-SHA256 token generation for cross-site patient deduplication, the same algorithm validated for public health surveillance use at 97% precision and 75% recall in published multi-site deployments.

Governance-aware architecture

Data use agreement alignment, suppression policy, stratification dimensions, and the separation between contributor-held data and published aggregates are built into the design from the start, not bolted on afterward.

Health equity, built in

Stratification by race, ethnicity, language, age, sex, homelessness, incarceration, and Medicaid enrollment is a core requirement of the design, not an optional layer, because those are exactly the populations this infrastructure exists to make visible.
