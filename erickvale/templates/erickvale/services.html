The Gap I Address

Health condition prevalence data in the United States mostly comes from survey methods that carry a two to three year lag, lack the geographic granularity local health departments need, and systematically underrepresent populations with severe and multiple disadvantages. People who have recently experienced homelessness or incarceration carry disproportionately high burdens of mental health, substance use, and chronic disease, and they are largely absent from the national data systems that inform healthcare policy and resource allocation.

Electronic health records, linked to administrative data on homelessness services, corrections involvement, Medicaid enrollment, and mortality, offer a path toward timely, population-level estimates for these groups. Building that path takes technical infrastructure: common data models, federated query architecture, privacy-preserving record linkage, and validated condition codesets that most public health agencies and community organizations cannot build alone.

That is the work I support.

Services

OMOP Common Data Model Implementation and Support

I support health systems and research networks in implementing and maintaining OMOP CDM infrastructure, including ETL pipeline development, vocabulary mapping, and standardizing demographic and encounter data across sites. I build condition codesets grounded in established sources, including the CMS Chronic Conditions Warehouse, and validate them through iterative clinical review, deploying OHDSI quality assurance tools including Achilles and the Data Quality Dashboard, plus custom scripts for longitudinal data quality monitoring.

Federated Network Architecture

I help organizations design and run distributed health data networks where patient-level data stays within each contributing site. That covers the governance structures, data use agreement frameworks, deduplication procedures, and analytic workflows that make multi-site collaboration work while preserving data security and institutional autonomy, drawing on established network models like PCORnet and HCSRN and adapting them to each jurisdiction.

Administrative Data Linkage

Characterizing the health of people experiencing homelessness or incarceration requires linking clinical data to administrative sources health systems do not control: Homeless Management Information System records, state department of corrections data, Medicaid enrollment files, immunization registries, vital statistics. I design and build privacy-preserving record linkage pipelines using hash-based algorithms that allow cross-source matching without transmitting personally identifiable information, plus the data enrichment workflows that augment OMOP records with these contextual variables.

Population Health Prevalence Estimation

I produce directly standardized, age and sex adjusted prevalence estimates for physical health, mental health, and substance use conditions, stratified by race and ethnicity, geography, and social determinants including homelessness, incarceration, and insurance status. Estimates go down to the state, county, zip code, and census tract level, the geographic resolution local health departments actually need to assess community health needs and allocate resources.

Data Product and Dashboard Development

I translate aggregated analytical results into publicly accessible dashboards, structured data exports, and interactive visualizations for public health practitioners, policymakers, and community organizations. I prioritize clarity, equity framing, and usability by non-technical audiences, and I build data products that update as new cohort data arrives instead of sitting there as one-time static outputs.

Grant Development, Technical Components

I support grant applications as a technical subcontractor or consultant, contributing data infrastructure plans, analytic frameworks, evaluation designs, and IRB-exempt public health surveillance justifications. I'm particularly familiar with CDC public health infrastructure grants, HRSA community health center programs, NIH observational research mechanisms, and the emerging CMS-mandated evaluation frameworks for Medicaid 1115 Reentry Waivers.

Community Health Needs Assessment Data Support

Hospitals and local health departments running CHNAs often lack access to condition prevalence data with enough timeliness, geographic resolution, or coverage of high-need populations. I provide CHNA-ready data packages with standardized prevalence estimates broken down by demographic group, geography, and special populations, with particular attention to people experiencing homelessness, incarceration, or Medicaid enrollment.
